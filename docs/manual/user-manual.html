Background

2. What LocalOps POS does

LocalOps POS is a till program that runs on one Windows computer. It works without the internet. All the information lives in a database file on that same computer.

It can

  • ring up sales for products and services;
  • take cash, card, EFT or “other” payments, including split payments;
  • print the receipt of the sale you have just finished;
  • record refunds and full voids without deleting anything;
  • track how much stock you have, and reduce it automatically when you sell;
  • open and close cash shifts and show you the variance;
  • record business expenses paid out of the drawer;
  • show sales, profit, payment and stock reports, and export them as a CSV file; and
  • make verified backups of the database and restore them.

Four rules that never change

RuleWhy it matters to you
Nothing is ever deletedA mistake is corrected by a refund or a void, and both the mistake and the correction stay in the history. This protects honest staff.
The money must balanceThe Pay button stays switched off until the amounts you enter add up exactly to the total.
A shift must be openNo shift, no sales. Opening the shift is how the system knows how much cash was in the drawer when you started.
The data stays hereThere is no cloud. If this computer is lost and there is no backup, the business history is lost. Make backups.

What this version cannot do

Do not promise a customer any of the following, because the screens do not exist yet:

  • customer accounts, credit sales, lay-by or quotations;
  • adding or editing staff accounts from inside the program (ask your administrator);
  • editing or deleting a product after it has been created;
  • re-printing an old receipt (print it at the moment of the sale);
  • selling by pack or crate at the till — the till sells whole catalogue items; and
  • sending backups to a USB drive or cloud from inside the program.

Every shift starts here

3. Signing in

When you start LocalOps POS on a computer that already has a business, you see the sign-in screen. Nothing can be done until you sign in with your own name and PIN.

  1. Check Business. It must be your shop’s name.
  2. Check Terminal. It must be the till you are standing at, for example Main Till.
  3. Type your Username in lower case, exactly as it was given to you.
  4. Type your PIN. It is 4 to 12 numbers. The screen shows dots, not the numbers.
  5. Press Sign in. The workspace opens.
Never do this

Never use another person’s username and PIN, and never tell anyone yours — not even a manager. Every sale, refund and expense is recorded under the name that is signed in. If you share your PIN, you are responsible for what someone else does with it.

Careful

After five wrong PINs the account is locked for five minutes. Wait; do not keep trying. Also check that you picked the right business and terminal before you blame the PIN.

Signing out

Sign out is the last button at the bottom of the left-hand menu. Press it every single time you walk away from the till, even for a minute. If the till is left alone for five minutes without activity, the session ends by itself and you must sign in again.

Tip

Signing out does not close the shift and does not lose any sale. A finished sale is saved the moment you press Pay.

Orientation

4. The parts of the screen

Every screen in LocalOps POS is built the same way: a strip at the top, a menu on the left, and the work area in the middle. Learn these three parts and you can find anything.

4.1 The top strip

Use the top strip to answer three questions in one glance: Am I on the right till? What is the time? Who is signed in? If the name is not yours, sign out and sign in again before you sell anything.

4.2 The left-hand menu

Menu itemWhat you do there
Order entrySell, take payment, print the receipt, refund or void.
CatalogueAdd products and services and see the item list.
InventoryStock on hand, receive stock, transfers, waste, counts.
Shift & expensesOpen and close the till, record expenses.
ReportsSales, profit, payments, stock value, CSV export.
Setup toolsCategories, units, packaging, recipes, which department sells what.
Backup & safetyHealth checks, make a backup, restore a backup.

A teller normally only uses Order entry and Shift & expenses. The other pages may open but refuse an action with a “Permission denied” message if your account is not allowed to do it. That is normal, not a fault.

4.3 Messages

The program talks to you with one wide bar above the work area:

  • Green — it worked. Example: “Sale PREVIEW-0001 completed. Receipt ready.”
  • Red — it did not work, and the bar says why. Example: “Preview: insufficient stock. Sale was not completed.”
Careful

When you see a red bar, read it and fix the cause. Do not press the button again and again — you may create the same transaction twice once the cause disappears.

First job of the day

5. Opening the till

A shift is the container for all the cash movement on this till. Until it is open, the sale screen shows a red Shift closed badge and nothing can be sold.

  1. Count the money that is physically in the drawer. Count it twice.
  2. Type the amount in Opening cash (ZAR). Use a full stop for cents: 500.00, not 500,00.
  3. Press Open shift. The badge at the top right turns green (Open shift) and the products appear.
Good habit

Enter the true counted amount, even if it is not the float you expected. If you enter a wrong opening amount, every cash figure for the whole shift will be wrong, and the variance at the end will blame the wrong person.

Tip

You can also open the shift from Shift & expenses. It is the same shift. One till can have only one shift open at a time.

Your main screen

The main job

7. Making a sale, step by step

7.1 Add the items

There are three ways to add an item. Use whichever is fastest:

WayHow
Tap the buttonPress the item on the grid. Press it again for a second one.
Scan the barcodePoint the scanner at the barcode. The item is added by itself. (The scanner types the number and presses Enter for you.)
Type and press EnterType the barcode, SKU or product code in the search bar and press Enter.
Careful

Typing a name and pressing Enter does not add an item. Enter only works on an exact barcode, SKU or product code. If nothing matches you see the red message “No local item matches …”. When that happens, clear the box and tap the item button instead.

7.2 Check the order

Fixing a line

  • – takes one off the line.
  • + adds one more.
  • Pressing – on a line with 1 removes the line.
  • The bin icon on the right removes the whole line at once.

Reading the totals

  • Subtotal — all the lines added up.
  • Tax — shows None if the business does not charge tax in the system.
  • Total due — the amount the customer must pay. Always read this number out loud to the customer.
Good habit

Before you say the price, look at the order panel and check the number of items at the top right of the panel against what is on the counter. Most mistakes are a double tap, not a wrong price.

Money

8. Taking payment

The rule is simple: what you type must add up to the Total due. When it does, the panel says Balanced and the big button turns on.

8.1 Cash, the fast way

  1. Press Exact cash. The cash box and the Cash tendered box are both filled with the total.
  2. If the customer hands you more money than the total, change Cash tendered to the amount they actually gave you, for example 300.00.
  3. Check that the panel says Balanced.
  4. Press Pay R ….
  5. Give the change that the receipt shows. Count it into the customer’s hand.

8.2 Card, EFT or other

  1. Complete the payment on the card machine first and make sure it is approved.
  2. Type the exact total in the box next to Card (or EFT / Other).
  3. Leave Cash on 0.00.
  4. Check Balanced, then press Pay R ….
Careful

You may not enter more than the total on a card, EFT or other line. Only cash may be over the amount, because only cash gives change.

8.3 Split payment (part cash, part card)

  1. Press Split payment / exact tender to open the boxes.
  2. Type the card part next to Card, for example 200.00.
  3. Type the cash part next to Cash, for example 50.00.
  4. Type what the customer physically gave you in Cash tendered, for example 100.00.
  5. The line under the boxes must read Allocated: R 250,00 and Balanced. If it says R … remaining, the amounts are short; if the button stays off, they are over.
  6. Press Pay R … and give the change.

8.4 Why the Pay button is grey

ReasonWhat to do
The order is emptyAdd at least one item.
No shift is openOpen the shift (chapter 5).
The amounts do not add upLook at the Allocated line and fix the numbers.
A box has bad text in itUse only digits and one full stop, with two decimals: 85.00.
The sale is busy savingWait. Never press twice.
Never do this

Never type a cash amount that the customer did not give you to make the sale “balance”. The drawer will be short at the end of the shift and the variance will point straight at your shift.

Proof of the sale

9. The receipt

The moment the sale is saved, a green bar confirms the receipt number and the receipt appears under the till.

  1. Read the total to the customer and hand over the change.
  2. Press Print receipt at the top right of the receipt.
  3. Choose the printer in the Windows window that opens, then press Print.
Careful

Print the receipt before you start the next customer. This version cannot re-print an old receipt from the History tab.

Good habit

If the printer jams or has no paper, the sale is still safely recorded. Fix the printer, then write the receipt number on a slip for the customer and explain politely.

Corrections

10. Refunds and voids

Use History to give money back. A refund returns part of a sale. A void reverses the whole sale. Neither one deletes anything: the original receipt stays exactly as it was and the reversal is added next to it.

10.1 Refunding part of a sale

  1. Open the History tab and press Open on the sale.
  2. Type a clear Reason for refund/void, for example “Wrong item, customer swapped”. It is compulsory.
  3. Under Items, set the quantity for every line. Put 0 on the lines you are not refunding.
  4. Switch Restock on only if the physical item came back and can be sold again. Switch it off for food that was eaten or anything damaged. Services can never be restocked.
  5. Under Payment reversals, type how much goes back to each method. Put 0.00 where nothing goes back. Normally you refund to the same method the customer paid with.
  6. Press Record refund.

10.2 Voiding a whole sale

  1. Open the sale from History.
  2. Type the reason, for example “Rung up on wrong till”.
  3. Check that every item quantity is the full quantity and that the payment amounts equal the full total.
  4. Set the Restock switches correctly.
  5. Press Void full sale.
If the screen refusesThe reason is usually
Buttons are greyNo shift is open, or the reason box is empty.
“Permission denied”Your account may not refund or void. Call a supervisor — do not use their PIN.
“exceeds refundable …”You are trying to give back more items or more money than is left on that sale. Check what was already refunded.
Never do this

Never refund cash for a card sale and never “fix” a mistake by ringing up a negative-looking sale. Always use the refund or void screen so the trail is honest and complete.

  2. Create your products and services in Catalogue.

Manager

12. Catalogue — products & services

The catalogue is the list of everything the business can sell. The three counters at the top tell you how many active items, products and services exist.

12.1 Adding a product

  1. Choose Product in the small list at the top right of the form.
  2. Name — what the teller will see on the button. Keep it short and clear.
  3. Price (ZAR) — the selling price, for example 25.00.
  4. Category — optional grouping for reports.
  5. Base unit — how you count it, usually EA — Each.
  6. Cost (ZAR) — what you pay for one. Used for profit reports.
  7. Barcode, SKU, Product code — optional, but a barcode makes scanning possible. Each value must be unique in the business.
  8. Track stock — leave on for anything physical.
  9. Taxable — set as your business requires.
  10. Press Add product.

12.2 Adding a service

Switch the list to Service, then type the name, price, optional category and optional duration in minutes, set Taxable, and press Add service. A service has no stock of its own, but it can eat stock through a recipe (for example, a treatment that uses 30 ml of product).

12.3 Reading the table

ColumnMeaning
ItemName and category.
TypeProduct or Service.
PriceThe catalogue price before any department override.
Local lookupBarcode · SKU · product code — what a scanner or the search bar can match.
Stock / durationTracked for stock items, or the service length in minutes.
CompositionStandard, or shows packaging and recipe information when set.
Careful

There is no edit or delete button in this version. Check the spelling and the price before you press Add.

Manager

13. Setup tools — categories, units, packaging, recipes

Setup tools is the workshop behind the catalogue. Five small panels, each with one job.

13.1 Categories

Type a Name, choose a Parent category (or leave it on Top level) and press Add category. Categories group items for browsing and reports, for example Drinks → Beer.

13.2 Units

A unit is how you measure an item. Each unit has a Code (KG), a Name (Kilogram), a Dimension (COUNT, WEIGHT, VOLUME, LENGTH or TIME), a scale as a fraction, and how many Decimals are allowed.

UnitDimensionScale NumScale DenDecimals
EA — EachCOUNT110
G — Gram (the base)WEIGHT113
KG — KilogramWEIGHT100013

Units that must convert into one another must share the same dimension. The program refuses a conversion that is not mathematically exact.

13.3 Product packaging

Packaging says how a buying unit converts to the selling unit — for example a crate that holds 12 bottles. Choose the Product, the Package unit, type a clear Name (Crate of 12), put 12 in Base-unit num and 1 in Base-unit den, then press Add packaging. Receiving 2 crates then adds 24 bottles to stock.

13.4 Recipe or consumable

A recipe takes ingredients out of stock when the item is sold.

  1. Choose the Sold item (a product or a service).
  2. Set the Recipe yield — use 1 when the ingredients make one sold unit.
  3. Choose Ingredient 1, its Quantity and its Unit.
  4. Press + Add ingredient for each extra ingredient.
  5. Press Save recipe.

Saving a new recipe replaces the old one from that moment forward. Sales that already happened keep the recipe that was used at the time.

13.5 Department availability

This is the step people forget. An item only appears on the till after you make it available:

  1. Choose the Department.
  2. Choose the Item.
  3. Optionally type a Price override — the price for that department only (happy-hour pricing, for example).
  4. Press Make available.

Stock controller

14. Inventory — stock control

Stock is kept as a ledger: a list of every movement that ever happened. The balance you see is the sum of that list, so it can always be explained.

14.1 The three counters

CounterMeaning
Tracked productsHow many products have stock tracking switched on.
Product/location balancesHow many product-and-place combinations exist.
Reconciliation differencesMust be 0. Anything else means a balance disagrees with the ledger — tell the owner and call support.

14.2 Add a supplier

Type the Name, optionally a Phone, and press Add supplier. Suppliers stay linked to the purchases you receive from them.

14.3 Receive a purchase

  1. Type a Purchase number that has not been used before (use the supplier invoice number).
  2. Choose the Supplier, or No supplier.
  3. Choose Receive into — the place the stock is going.
  4. Choose the Product and the Purchase unit (the base unit, or a packaging such as Crate of 12).
  5. Type the Quantity and the Unit cost (ZAR) for that purchase unit.
  6. Optionally type an Invoice reference, and set Payment status to Unpaid, Part paid or Paid.
  7. Press Receive stock.

One press creates the purchase, updates the product’s latest cost, writes the stock movement and updates the balance. If anything is wrong, nothing at all is saved.

14.4 Transfer, waste and counting

FormHow to use it
Transfer stockChoose From and a different To location, the product and the quantity, then Complete transfer. It stays switched off while the business has only one location.
Waste or damageChoose the location and product, type the quantity lost and a clear Reason, switch on Physical damage if the item was broken, then Record loss.
Stock countChoose the location and product, type the Counted quantity you physically counted and press Complete count. The system keeps the expected figure and posts only the difference.

14.5 Recent movements

The table at the bottom is the truth behind every balance: time, product, location, movement type, quantity and reference. A positive quantity adds stock; a negative quantity takes it away.

Never do this

Never invent a purchase to get past an “insufficient stock” message. Receive the real stock or do an authorised stock count. False receipts destroy the value of every report that follows.

Cash control

15. Shifts & expenses

This page is where the drawer is opened, where money paid out of the drawer is recorded, and where the shift is counted and closed.

15.1 The three figures

FigureMeaning
Opening cashWhat you counted into the drawer when the shift opened.
Expected cash nowOpening cash + cash sales − cash refunds − cash expenses. This is what should be in the drawer at this moment.
Shift openedThe time the shift started.

15.2 Recording an expense

You need at least one expense category and an open shift first. To create a category, type a name such as Transport and press Create category.

  1. Choose the Category.
  2. Choose the Department, or business-wide.
  3. Choose the Payment method — pick Cash only when money really left the drawer.
  4. Type the Amount.
  5. Check the Date.
  6. Type a useful Description (“Taxi to collect gas bottle”) and an optional Reference.
  7. Press Record expense.

A cash expense lowers Expected cash now immediately. Other methods do not touch the drawer.

15.3 Voiding an expense

Find the entry in Expense history, press Void, type the reason and confirm. The entry stays visible with a void status — financial history is voided, never deleted.

15.4 Closing and counting the shift

  1. Stop serving customers on this till.
  2. Take the drawer to a safe place and count all the cash.
  3. Type the true figure in Actual cash counted.
  4. Add Closing notes if something needs explaining.
  5. Press Close shift.

The shift history row then keeps the opening, expected, actual and variance figures for ever.

VarianceMeaningWhat to do
R 0,00Perfect.Nothing.
Positive (over)More cash than expected — change not given, or a sale rung up short.Write a note and tell your supervisor.
Negative (short)Less cash than expected — too much change, a missed sale, or an unrecorded payout.Recount, check expenses, then report it the same day.
Good habit

Count the drawer without looking at the expected figure first. Then compare. This keeps the count honest.

Owner / manager

16. Reports

The business dashboard answers: what did we sell, what did we earn, what did we spend, and what is still on the shelf?

16.1 Choosing the period

Set From and To, then press Refresh. The dashboard opens on the first day of this month up to today. The badge at the bottom right always repeats the range you are looking at.

16.2 What the cards mean

CardMeaning
Net salesSales after refunds and voids.
Estimated gross profitSales less the recorded cost of the items sold. It is an estimate for management, not a financial statement.
ExpensesOperating expenses recorded in the period.
Refunds & voidsMoney given back in the period.
Stock valueWhat is on the shelf right now, valued at the latest cost.
Low-stock balancesHow many product balances are at or under their minimum.
Gross sales (bottom)Sales before refunds.
Shift variance (bottom)All shift variances in the period added together.

16.3 The tables

  • Departments — gross, refunds, net and estimated gross profit per department.
  • Payments — received, refunded and net per payment method. Compare the Cash net figure with your drawer counts.
  • Current stock valuation — on hand, minimum and value per product and location.

16.4 Export CSV

Choose the range, press Export CSV and save the file. It is named localops-report-YYYY-MM-DD-to-YYYY-MM-DD.csv and opens in any spreadsheet program. Treat the file as confidential business information.

Protection

17. Backup & safety

There is no cloud copy. This page is the only thing standing between the business and a lost hard drive.

17.1 Reading the health cards

CardHealthy value
SQLite integrityok
Foreign-key violations0
Stock differences0
Unaudited financial events0
SchemaCurrent version equals the latest version, e.g. v6 (latest v6)
Overall stateHealthy
Never do this

Never ignore a card that is not healthy, and never “try things” to fix it. Write the exact values down, make a backup if the button still works, stop non-essential work and call support.

17.2 Making a backup

  1. Open Backup & safety.
  2. Check that the overall state is Healthy.
  3. Press Create manual backup.
  4. Wait for the green “Verified backup created” message.
  5. Check that the new row in the table says Verified.

The program also keeps automatic copies by itself: 7 daily, 4 weekly and 12 monthly. Manual and pre-restore copies are kept separately from those.

17.3 Restoring a backup

Careful

Restoring replaces the current data with the data from the backup. Everything recorded after that backup is not in the restored copy. Only do this with the owner’s instruction.

  1. Stop all selling on the computer.
  2. Find the verified backup you need in the list.
  3. Press Restore and read the confirmation carefully before approving.
  4. The program makes a pre-restore safety copy first, then restores, signs you out and reloads.
  5. Sign in with the credentials that existed at the time of that backup.

17.4 Data rules for everybody

  1. Do not put the database on a shared network drive.
  2. Do not open business.db with any other program.
  3. Do not delete the -wal or -shm files while LocalOps is running.
  4. Close LocalOps before an administrator copies backups to external storage.
  5. Do not switch the computer off during a sale, a restore or an upgrade.
  6. Treat exported CSV files and backup copies as confidential.

MessageWhat to do

This means either nothing has been created in Catalogue, or nothing has been made available to a department in Setup tools. Both steps are needed. It is almost always the second one.

18.3 Other common problems

  • the receipt, purchase or reference number involved; and
  • the values on the Backup & safety cards, if you can open that page.

For trainers

19. Trainer’s guide

A new teller can be trained on LocalOps POS in about 45 minutes, plus practice. Train on a real till, out of trading hours, and refund or void every practice sale afterwards.

19.1 Lesson plan (45 minutes)

TimeTopicDo this
0–5 minThe four rulesChapter 2. Explain that nothing is deleted and that everything is recorded under their name.
5–10 minSign in, sign outLet them sign in themselves. Make them sign out and in again twice.
10–15 minScreen tourPoint at the top strip, the menu and the order panel. Ask them to point back.
15–20 minOpen the tillCount a real float together and open a shift.
20–32 minSalesExercises 1 to 4 below.
32–38 minRefundsExercise 5. Explain Restock properly.
38–45 minClose the shiftCount, close, look at the variance together.

19.2 Practice exercises

  1. Simple cash sale. Two of one item, one of another. Exact cash. Print the receipt.
  2. Change. Total R 85,00, customer gives R 100,00. Enter the tender correctly and hand over the change.
  3. Card sale. One item on card. Show that cash must stay on 0.00.
  4. Split payment. R 250,00 as R 200,00 card and R 50,00 cash, with R 100,00 tendered.
  5. Refund one line. Refund only one item from exercise 1, with a written reason, Restock on.
  6. Mistake drill. Add a wrong item on purpose, then remove it with the bin icon before paying.
